A business card website is a small site about a company or a specialist: what you do, what it costs, what work you have done and how to reach you. It is usually one long page or a few short ones, up to five to seven. For a new business it is a sensible first site: it takes 1-2 weeks, costs several times less than a corporate site, and you can put its address on business cards, in your map listing and on social media right away.

Below we look at why a business card website is enough at the start, when it is not, what to put on it and how it grows with the business. In the middle there are examples of business card websites from our own work. If you want the price straight away, see the [business card website](/services/vizitka) page.

## Why a business card website is enough at the start

**You do not know yet which pages you will need.** The first months show which services sell, what clients ask on the phone and where they come from. A big site built before that rests on guesses, and some of its pages get rewritten later. A business card website is easier to extend once you have the answers.

**Clients check you before they call.** Someone sees an ad, gets your card or a recommendation and searches for your name. If they find a site with prices, work and contacts, calling you is an easy next step. A social media page helps too, but there you live by the platform's rules. The domain and the site belong to you.

**The site starts building a history.** After launch you can add it to [Yandex Webmaster](/blog/yandex-webmaster), search engines start indexing it, and Metrica collects the first data about visitors. When it is time to work on search, you will be looking at several months of figures.

**The budget goes into the work.** With us a business card website costs from $200 and a corporate site from $1,000. At the start the difference is more useful in advertising, materials or your first orders.

## When a business card website is not enough

- **You are launching ads for one service right away.** A landing page suits ads better: it leads to one action and is easier to match to the ad. How a landing page differs from a business card website is covered in [what is a landing page](/blog/what-is-a-landing-page), and how to build one for ads in our article on a [landing page for targeted ads and Yandex Direct](/blog/landing-page-for-ads).
- **The company has many lines of work and needs search traffic for many queries.** One page cannot answer dozens of queries. For example, the engineering company we recently built a [site](https://sk-tps.ru/) for has five lines of work and separate pages for 28 towns: that is already a [corporate website](/services/corporate).
- **You sell goods with a cart and online payment.** That calls for an [online store](/services/ecommerce).

## Types of business card websites

- **A specialist's personal site.** A psychologist, tutor, lawyer or photographer. The main subject is you: experience, approach, reviews and booking a consultation.
- **A craftsman's or studio's site.** Repairs, beauty, custom furniture. The main things are photos of work, prices and a way to book.
- **A small company's site.** Manufacturing, services, delivery. The main things are what you do, for whom, documents, examples and sales contacts.

The type decides what goes on the first screen, but the set of sections is similar for all of them.

## What to put on a business card website

1. **The first screen.** Who you are, what you do and where you work, plus a contact button. A visitor should get this in a few seconds.
2. **Services and prices.** At least "from" prices. If you cannot name a price in advance, explain what it depends on.
3. **Examples of work.** Photos, projects, results. Five good examples convince better than twenty average ones.
4. **Why people can trust you.** Experience, documents, licences, reviews. Only what can be checked: people sense made-up numbers.
5. **Contacts.** Phone, messengers, email, address and map, opening hours. On a phone a messenger button is often more convenient than a form.
6. **A request form.** Name and phone, with no extra fields. If you collect names and phone numbers, you need consent to personal data processing and a privacy policy.

Three more things visitors do not think about, although without them the site works at half strength:

- **A mobile version.** People usually come to the site from a paper business card or a map listing on their phone, and there it has to be readable without zooming. What to check is described in our article on a [responsive website](/blog/responsive-website).
- **Yandex Metrica with goals.** It shows how many people sent a request or tapped the phone number. How to set it up is in our article on [Metrica goals](/blog/yandex-metrika-goals).
- **A domain in your name.** Then the address stays with you even if you change contractors. How to choose one is covered in our article on a [domain for a website](/blog/domain-for-website).

Today it has more than twenty service pages and about twenty articles. We added each section once it was clear why it was needed.

## How to grow out of a business card website

A business card website becomes too small gradually, and the signs are easy to see:

- clients keep asking the same questions on the phone, so it is time to add an FAQ block or a separate page;
- one service brings most of the requests, so it needs its own page or a landing page for ads;
- you want clients from search for different queries, so you need a page for each service and possibly a blog.

To keep growth from turning into a rebuild, agree on three things when you launch. The domain and hosting are in your name. Metrica is connected from day one, so the data history does not start over. The structure allows adding pages: it is better to lay out services as separate blocks that can later move to their own addresses. Then a year later new pages are added to the same site, and you do not have to rebuild it from scratch.
