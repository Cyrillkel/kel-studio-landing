There are three ways to create a Telegram bot for business: put it together on a bot builder, order it from a developer or write it yourself. A builder suits simple scenarios, a developer is needed for a bot with payment, integrations and an admin panel, and writing it yourself is for those who can program. Below: what a bot can do in business, how it works, how the ways differ and how the work on a custom bot goes.

## What a bot can do in business

Clients do not need to install anything: it is enough to open the chat and press "Start". After that the bot can:

- take requests and bookings and pass them to you in Telegram, by email or into a CRM;
- answer frequent questions: address, schedule, prices, what the service includes;
- show a catalog of goods or services and place an order;
- accept payment and send clients notifications;
- send mailings and reminders to those who subscribed to the bot themselves;
- keep statistics: who wrote and what they ordered.

An example from our own practice: requests from the form on this site reach us in Telegram through our own bot. We see them at once, and the form is protected from spam without making the client solve a captcha.

## How a bot works technically

A bot is a program that runs on a server and talks to Telegram through the official Bot API. First a record for the new bot is created with the official bot @BotFather, and it issues a token, that is, a key with which the program controls the bot. The token is kept secret: with it anyone can write on behalf of the bot.

The program has to run somewhere around the clock: on hosting or on your own server. Builders take this on themselves. Details for developers are collected in the [Telegram documentation](https://core.telegram.org/bots).

## Three ways to create a bot

| Way | Suits | Strengths | Limits |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bot builder | requests, answers to frequent questions, a simple menu | launch in hours or days, no programmer needed | limited scenarios and integrations, the bot and data live on the service's platform, often a subscription |
| A developer to order | payment, a catalog, integrations with a CRM and spreadsheets, an admin panel | any scenarios, the data is yours, the bot can be developed further | more expensive and slower, a brief is needed |
| By yourself | a test bot, and those who can program | full flexibility | time for code, hosting and maintenance, and the token has to be protected |

A simple request and a couple of buttons fit into a builder. If the bot needs payment, a link to a CRM, its own rules and a panel where you change texts and prices yourself, it is better to order development. Changing the platform after launch is harder than choosing a suitable one at once.

## How the work on a custom bot goes

1. **Brief and scenarios.** We find out what the bot should do and who will use it, and describe the dialogue scenarios.
2. **Dialogue prototype.** We show a scheme of the menu and messages and agree on texts and buttons.
3. **Development.** We write the bot and connect payment, integrations and the admin panel.
4. **Testing.** We go through all the scenarios and check notifications and error handling.
5. **Launch and support.** We launch the bot, hand over the access and, if needed, support it.

We name the price and timeline after the brief and fix them in the contract.

## A bot or a Mini App

A bot holds a dialogue in a chat: buttons and messages. A [Mini App](/services/telegram-mini-apps) is a full interface inside Telegram, similar to a website or an application. For requests, answers to questions and reminders a bot is enough. A catalog with a cart and a personal account is more convenient as a mini app. They are often combined: the bot carries the conversation, and the mini app opens from a button.

A bot does not replace a website. A site brings the client from search and advertising, a bot takes the request and keeps in touch. How to choose and create a site is covered in the article [how to create a website for business](/blog/how-to-create-a-website).

## What to prepare before you order

- One task the bot should solve in the first month.
- The texts of answers to the ten most frequent client questions.
- A decision on where requests go, who answers them and within what time.
- Whether payment in the bot is needed or a request is enough.
- The bot's name, avatar and description.
- The text of consent to personal data processing, if you collect phone numbers and names.

## Common mistakes

**A bot with no task.** The wording "like the competitors have" tells the contractor nothing. Name the specific job the bot will take over.

**Too many buttons.** The client gets lost on the third level of the menu. Start with three to five items.

**No handover to a person.** If the bot does not know the answer to a question, there should be a button to contact a manager.

**No admin panel.** Then every text edit goes through the developer.

**A bot with no audience.** Subscribers have to be brought in: a link on the site, a QR code on a business card, a pinned message in social networks.
