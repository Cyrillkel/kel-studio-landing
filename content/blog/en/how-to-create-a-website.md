There are five ways to create a business website: build it on a website builder, install a ready-made CMS, order it from a studio, order it from a freelancer, or get a draft from an AI tool. The ways differ in budget, timeline and in what you own in the end. Below, in order: how they differ, which types of sites exist, what stages the work consists of, what it costs and what to prepare before you order.

We are a studio, so we have a stake in this. To keep that from skewing the picture, the prices come from our public [prices](/prices) page, and the table says where another option fits better.

## Five ways to create a website

| Way | Suits | Strengths | Limits |
|---|---|---|---|
| Website builder | a business card site or a simple landing page, testing an idea | launch in hours or days, minimal budget | the platform's limits, a monthly subscription, ownership depends on the service's terms |
| A ready-made CMS, for example [WordPress](/services/wordpress) | a business card site, a corporate site, a blog, a small store | full ownership, plenty of ready-made themes and extensions | hosting and maintenance are on you: updates, backups, protection |
| Studio | a site with an individual design, integrations and support | a team of designer, developer and manager, a contract, after-launch support | costs more than a single contractor |
| Freelancer | a small or medium site with a clear brief | flexible price, direct contact with the person doing the work | everything rests on one person: a vacation, illness or silence breaks the deadlines |
| AI tool | a quick draft of a page or a text | speed and low price | the result has to be checked and finished, and integrations, search and support stay with you |

The choice starts with the task. A business card site for a workshop is fine on a builder or a ready-made theme. If you need a separate page for every service, non-standard logic or requests passed to a CRM, it is cheaper to start with custom development than to move from platform to platform later. The first two options are covered in detail in the article [website builder or custom development](/blog/website-builder-or-custom).

## What stages the work consists of

We go through six steps:

1. **Brief and analysis.** The goal of the site, the audience, competitors' sites.
2. **Structure and prototype.** Which pages and blocks are needed and in what order they go.
3. **Design.** Layouts for computer and phone.
4. **Development.** Page layout, request forms, integrations, the admin panel.
5. **Testing and launch.** Checking on devices, domain, hosting, secure connection, analytics.
6. **Support and growth.** Updates, edits, attracting visitors.

Texts and photos are prepared in parallel. If you do not have them, allow time to prepare them: an empty site cannot be launched.

An example from our own practice: after launching the studio's site we connected [Yandex Metrica](https://metrica.yandex.com/) with goals and [Yandex Webmaster](/blog/yandex-webmaster). Now we can see where requests come from and which pages made it into search. Without this step a site works blind.

## What to budget for besides development

**Domain and hosting.** Paid regularly. Register the domain in your own name: it is your address on the internet, and you will not have to change it if you switch contractors. How to choose the name and the zone is covered in our article on [a domain for a website](/blog/domain-for-website), and the kinds of servers and how to choose one in the article on [hosting](/blog/website-hosting).

**Support.** Updates, backups, edits after launch. The price depends on the platform, the size of the site and the amount of work, so we price [support](/services/support) after getting to know the site.

**Attracting visitors.** A site does not bring clients by itself; advertising and search do. [Yandex Direct management](/services/yandex-direct) with us starts from $200, and the advertising budget is paid separately. [SEO promotion](/services/seo) starts from $200, and the first shifts are usually visible after 2-3 months. Budget for this from the start: a site without visitors stands empty.

## What to prepare before you order

- What the site should do: take requests, sell, tell about the company.
- Who your clients are and where they come to you from.
- Two or three sites you like and one you do not.
- Logo, photos, texts. If you do not have them, [design](/services/design) and texts can be ordered separately.
- Access to the domain and hosting, if you already have them.
- Who in your company approves the result. When several people make decisions, timelines grow.

If you do not have answers to some of this, that is fine: we work out the structure and scope together with you at the brief.

## Common mistakes

**Starting with design instead of the task.** A beautiful page with no clear goal leaves the visitor wondering what to do next.

**Adding features "for growth".** A personal account, three languages and a blog are not needed on day one. Launch what you need to take requests and add the rest later.

**Registering the domain and hosting in the contractor's name.** If you part ways, getting the access back will be hard. Everything is registered in your name.

**Not checking the site on a phone.** A noticeable share of visitors come from smartphones, and an awkward page loses requests.

**Not connecting analytics.** Without a counter you cannot tell whether the site works.

**Expecting clients right after launch.** Search needs time and advertising needs a budget.
