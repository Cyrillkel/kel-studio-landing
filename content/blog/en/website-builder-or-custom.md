A website builder and custom development solve the same task: the business gets a website. The difference is whose limits you get along with the result. With a builder these are the limits of the platform; with custom development they are budget and timelines. Below we look at when a builder is enough, when it starts to get in the way and how to choose without overpaying.

We build custom sites and WordPress sites, so a caveat up front: a builder suits many people, and we will not talk you into custom development where it is not needed.

## What a website builder is

A builder is a service where a site is put together from ready-made blocks in the browser: you pick a template, change the texts and pictures, connect a domain and publish. Hosting, the editor and updates are included in the subscription. No programming is needed, and the first version is ready in an evening or two.

You pay a subscription while the site is running, and you live inside the platform's capabilities. What the platform cannot do will not be on your site.

## Where a builder helps

- **You need to test an idea quickly and cheaply.** A page for testing demand should not cost more than the experiment itself.
- **The site is small and simple.** A few pages, a request form, contacts, an address on a map.
- **You will change the texts yourself.** The visual editor was made for exactly this.
- **The starting budget is almost zero.** A subscription costs less than development, and when the site stops being enough for you, it can be replaced.

For a small service, an event or a specialist's personal page this is a normal choice. A good builder site is better than no site.

## Where a builder starts to get in the way

The limits usually show up later: when the site already brings requests and you want more.

**Loading speed.** A builder usually loads its own set of scripts and styles on every page, even if you use a small part of it. On a phone this is noticeable, and speed affects both search and whether the visitor waits for the page to load.

**Search promotion.** Most builders can set up headings, descriptions, page addresses and a sitemap. It gets harder with what goes beyond the template: a non-standard structure of sections, data markup, fine-tuning of indexing. If the site has to bring clients from search, check these options before choosing a platform.

**Integrations and logic.** Requests into a CRM, price calculation on the page, a personal account, synchronization with an accounting system. Simple things exist as ready-made widgets, and everything else runs into what the platform allows.

**Owning the site.** The site lives on the platform, and it is not always possible to move it out whole. Before building anything important on a builder, open the service's terms and find out whether the site can be exported and in what form. Also find out what happens to the site if you stop paying or the plan changes.

**Cost in the long run.** The subscription is small, but it is paid every month or year while you need the site. Over three to five years the sum becomes comparable to a one-off development.

## When you need custom development

Development is justified when the site is directly tied to revenue or has to do something the ready-made blocks do not:

- the site has to attract clients from search and you plan to invest in promotion;
- you need non-standard logic: a calculator, a personal account, a catalog with filters, a link to a CRM or an accounting system;
- the company has several lines of business and each needs its own page with a separate structure;
- loading speed matters and you want to control the code;
- the site must belong to you entirely: code, data, hosting.

A custom site can be developed for years without changing the platform. You pay for this with a one-off budget and with the fact that the launch takes weeks, not an evening.

## The middle ground: WordPress

Between a builder and a site from scratch there is a [WordPress site](/services/wordpress). It is a ready-made management system with a huge choice of themes and extensions that can be deployed on your own hosting. You change texts and pages yourself, as in a builder, and the site belongs to you and any specialist can maintain it.

An example from our practice: for a clothing factory we made a business card site on WordPress with a ready-made theme and Elementor. It needed information about the production, examples of work and contacts. Writing such a site from scratch would have meant wasting someone else's money, so we honestly suggested a ready-made base.

## Comparison by the main criteria

| | Builder | WordPress | Custom development |
|---|---|---|---|
| Launch | from a few hours to a couple of days | depends on the type of site | landing page 1-2 weeks, corporate site 3-5 weeks |
| Starting budget | minimal | medium | higher |
| Payments afterwards | subscription | hosting and support | hosting and support |

| Non-standard logic | limited by the platform | by extensions | any |
| Owning the site | depends on the service's terms | full | full |
| Who changes the texts | you | you | you, if an admin panel is built |

## Five questions to choose by

1. **Does the site need to bring clients from search?** If so, choose WordPress or development: they give more control over speed and structure.
2. **Do you need anything besides pages and a request form?** A calculator, a personal account, payment, a link to an accounting system almost always mean development.
3. **How many pages will there be in a year?** Five - a builder is enough. Fifty, with a separate page for each service, and you will need a more serious system.
4. **How important is owning the site to you?** If it is the main sales channel, it is better to have the code, data and hosting in your hands.
5. **How much are you ready to invest now and how much later?** A builder is cheap today, but the subscription goes on every year. Development costs more today and less later.

## What suits whom

- **A beginning specialist or a craftsman** who needs a page with contacts: a builder.
- **A small business with one or two services** that needs a site for advertising: a custom [landing page](/services/landing), or a well-made one on a builder if you are ready to set it up yourself.
- **A company with several lines of business** that wants to attract clients from search: a [corporate website](/services/corporate) on WordPress or a custom one.
- **A store or a service with personal accounts**: development, because a platform will quickly become too tight.
